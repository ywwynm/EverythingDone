# Water Visual-Quality Research — Techniques for a Calm, Identity-Colored 2.5D Pond (AGSL / per-vertex)

Research date: 2026-07. Scope: make the existing 9-layer + continuous-heightfield pond (~280×420dp, ~3000 verts, 25×~120 grid, AGSL fragment stage) feel materially richer — light, color depth, motion, edges, finish — under hard constraints: single identity hue (solid or 2-stop gradient), calm small-pond scale, AGSL limits (no dFdx/dFdy, no texture mips, compile-time-bounded loops), and the project's red lines (no sharp spikes, no jittery level, no transient-audio reshaping of waves, shadows keep identity hue, far darkness faint). Already-rejected items (caustics, grey contact shadows, low-quality screen particles, three-segment optical compositing + refraction parallax, static reduced-motion) are **not** re-proposed; the paused glitter light-pillar is only discussed as physics that informs other choices.

## Q1. Light on small calm water — glint statistics, sparkle anti-aliasing, glitter-path physics, sky gradients

### Cox–Munk slope statistics (the "physics budget" for glints)
- Cox & Munk measured sea-surface slope PDFs from sun-glitter photographs; the slope distribution is a **bivariate Gaussian** in along-wind/cross-wind slope with variances growing ~linearly with wind speed: σa² ≈ 3.16×10⁻³·U, σc² ≈ 0.003 + 1.92×10⁻³·U, total σ² ≈ 0.003 + 5.12×10⁻³·U (U in m/s). At pond-relevant winds (1–5 m/s) total mean-square slope is only **0.008–0.029** — i.e. RMS slopes of ~5°–10°. This is the quantitative anchor for "calm": glint-capable facets are rare and shallow. Source: Ocean Optics Web Book — https://www.oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces/cox-munk-sea-surface-slope-statistics
- Key anisotropy: **along-wind slope variance exceeds cross-wind variance** (σa² > σc² above ~2 m/s). A glint field driven by these two variances is automatically slightly anisotropic — one principled, subtle way to orient light with the scene's implied wind without any "pillar" geometry.
- NOAA's outreach page (and Shaw's OPN paper) give the glitter-pattern geometry rules: pattern is **roughly elliptical, width:length = sin(source elevation)**; angular length ≈ **4× the maximum wave slope** (factor 2 from toward/away facets × factor 2 from reflection angle doubling); glints "come and go" and **blend into a smooth path only at distance**. Sources: https://psl.noaa.gov/outreach/education/science/glitter/ , https://www.montana.edu/jshaw/documents/Glitter_patterns_OPN1999.pdf
- Why the glitter path looks like a **parallel-sided vertical column** rather than a triangle: as one looks farther away, the physical width of glint-capable water grows, but pinhole projection compresses width by 1/distance — the two effects cancel, leaving constant image width (arXiv, "Light pillars over rippled water: why their sides appear parallel," 2026): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18547 — Useful even with the pillar paused: it says **elongation should live along the depth/Z axis of the pond (screen-vertical in a 2.5D ribbon), never horizontally**, and that width variation should follow local roughness, not distance.
- Painter's observational rules (James Gurney, citing Krøyer): the path **widens where water is disturbed, narrows where calm**; it is made of "spots or dashes," not a continuous streak. https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2019/03/glitter-path.html — a direct art-direction rule: let glint density/width follow the cat's-paw/gust field already in the app.

### Sparkle rendering without aliasing
- **Journey's glitter** (Alan Zucconi's 5-part analysis of thatgamecompany's technique): each texel gets a stable random micro-normal G; compute `R = reflect(L, G)`; a sparkle fires only when `dot(R, V)` passes a **threshold** — rare and very bright, with values >1 feeding bloom. Critically, the mapping is **deterministic per surface point**, so sparkles are anchored in place and don't flicker as things move — the same "persistent tracked entity" philosophy the app already uses for glints (validation + refinement source). https://www.alanzucconi.com/2019/10/08/journey-sand-shader-5/ ; GDC talk: https://gdcvault.com/play/1017742/Sand-Rendering-in
- **Zirr & Kaplanyan, "Real-time Rendering of Procedural Multiscale Materials" (I3D 2016)**: procedural sub-pixel glints with a **biscale NDF** and a scale hierarchy that estimates pixel footprint per LOD for temporal stability and anti-aliasing — the canonical "sparkle without shimmer" reference; a simplified single-scale version (bounded loop over a few stable hash cells, smooth window in glint parameter space) is AGSL-expressible. Paper: https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2016-02_Real-time-Rendering-of/ZirrKaplanyan_MultiscaleI3D2016.pdf ; demo: https://www.shadertoy.com/view/ldVGRh ; follow-up procedural glint BRDF (Chermain et al. 2020): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.14141
- **Kaplanyan's GDC 2016 specular AA**: shading aliasing comes from specular highlights + normal variation; filter the NDF in slope space using the pixel-footprint normal variance. Without dFdx, the app can compute that variance **analytically** from its known wave sum (see Q3). https://developer.download.nvidia.com/gameworks/events/GDC2016/akaplanyan_specular_aa.pdf

### Sky-reflection gradients on low-amplitude ripples
- Fresnel: near-vertical viewing reflects only ~2%, grazing angles >60–80% — so on a 2.5D ribbon the **far rows should carry much more sky and the near rows much more body color**; calm water "further away is more reflective." Sources: https://freshscientific.org/reflection-on-water-physics , https://unitywatershader.wordpress.com/2018/05/14/sky-reflections-and-fresnel/
- Independent **perspective brightening toward the horizon**: farther water packs a wider physical extent of sky reflection into the same visual angle, so the reflection brightens toward the far edge even before Fresnel (Minnaert-style analysis): https://pscolour.eu/English/horizon.htm
- What makes real pond light read as "expensive" (synthesis of the above sources): (1) glints obey slope statistics — sparse, sub-degree facets, density modulated by local roughness patches; (2) highlight stretching is strictly axis-aligned with view depth; (3) the sky term is a smooth vertical gradient with Fresnel + perspective compounding; (4) roughness patches (cat's paws) *simultaneously* darken the sky term and widen the glint field — coupling the two is the tell of physical truth (cat's paws darken because steeper ripples reflect sky away from the eye: https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2017/11/cats-paws-on-water/ , https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Oceanography_101_(Miracosta)/10:_Waves/10.08:_Cat's_Paws ). The app has both systems; **coupling them through one shared roughness field** is the upgrade.

## Q2. Subsurface-scattering approximations for stylized water

- **Sea of Thieves (SIGGRAPH 2018 talk, Rare)** — the canonical stylized-SSS recipe: water color "based on scattering approximations"; they **blend a deep-water color and a sub-surface color** using (a) view angle, (b) sun direction, (c) a **wave-peak mask derived from FFT choppiness offsets** — larger horizontal choppiness ⇒ wave peak ⇒ shorter light path ⇒ more subsurface color. They also use a Karis closest-point-on-sphere **area specular** for a wide low-sun reflection. PDF: https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf (DOI: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3214745.3214820 ). Transfer: the app's Gerstner-style orbits already produce a per-vertex "pinch" (horizontal convergence) — the analog of the choppiness mask, free to compute.
- **Crest ocean (open source)** — exact shader scaffold in `OceanEmission.hlsl`:
  - base: `col = lerp(_DiffuseGrazing, _Diffuse, abs(view.y))`
  - sun SSS: `towardsSun = pow(max(0, dot(lightDir, -view)), _SubSurfaceSunFallOff)`; `subsurface = (_SubSurfaceBase + _SubSurfaceSun * towardsSun) * _SubSurfaceColour * lightCol`, attenuated by `(1 - view.y²)` so it lives at grazing angles.
  https://github.com/wave-harmonic/crest/blob/master/crest/Assets/Crest/Crest/Shaders/OceanEmission.hlsl ; parameter docs (SSS Intensity Base / Sun / Sun Falloff / Tint): https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/water-appearance.html
- **Wrap lighting** (GPU Gems ch. 16): `diffuse = max(0, (dot(N,L) + w) / (1 + w))`, w∈[0,1]; softens the terminator and reads as translucency; the classic cheap SSS. https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-16-real-time-approximations-subsurface-scattering — For energy-conserving variants and pitfalls see Steve McAuley's wrap analysis: https://blog.selfshadow.com/2011/12/31/righting-wrap-part-1/ and the original jgt note: https://cim.mcgill.ca/~derek/files/jgt_wrap.pdf ; Zucconi's fast-SSS series adds the back-translucency term `pow(saturate(dot(V, -(L + N*δ))), p)`: https://www.alanzucconi.com/2017/08/30/fast-subsurface-scattering-1/
- **Gentle (non-tropical) parameter guidance** distilled from these models: keep the *base* (omnidirectional) SSS small relative to the sun term (Crest exposes exactly this split); use **high falloff exponents (≈4–10)** so the effect concentrates in a soft lobe rather than washing the whole surface; drive intensity by crest thinness (pinch mask), not by audio transients (red-line safe); and tint the SSS color as an **OKLab-lightened, slightly-greened variant of the identity hue** rather than a fixed teal — tropical looks come from saturated cyan SSS tints + low falloff, calm looks from small, hue-faithful lifts. (Parameter framing per Crest docs above; hue derivation per Q4.)

## Q3. Beautiful per-pixel normals from a coarse heightfield, without shimmer

- **Analytic normals from the wave sum** (GPU Gems ch. 1, "Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models"): for height = Σ Aᵢ sin(ωᵢ(Dᵢ·x) + φᵢt), the slope is the analytic partial-derivative sum ("derivative of a sum is the sum of derivatives"); normals from cross(T,B). Also: deep-water dispersion ω=√(gk); crest sharpening via `(sin+1)^k` exponent; Gerstner steepness Q. Analytic beats finite differences in both cost and quality at 25-row resolution — finite differences on a 25×216 grid low-pass the very slopes that make light interesting. https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models
- **Analytic derivatives of procedural noise** (Inigo Quilez, "morenoise"): value/gradient noise can return its 2D/3D gradient in the same evaluation (polynomial interpolants have closed-form derivatives); fbm derivatives are the weighted sum of octave derivatives; derivative feedback (`1/(1+dot(d,d))` weighting) yields natural flat-vs-rough patchiness — a beautiful, cheap way to make micro-ripple fields patchy like real water instead of uniformly noisy. https://iquilezles.org/articles/morenoise/ — AGSL-feasible: hash via sin-free integer hashing, fixed 2–3 octaves (compile-time loops), no textures needed.
- **Band-limiting without dFdx**: fade any octave whose wavelength approaches the pixel/row footprint and replace it with its mean; footprint is known analytically on a fixed-projection ribbon (row height in px is known per row), so "fade octaves by row index" replaces derivative-based filtering. Practice references: https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/673199-desperate-antialiasingfiltering-of-procedural-texture/ ; research framing (mean+variance program smoothing): https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.01208
- **Specular AA (Toksvig/CLEAN, simplified for mobile)**: Toksvig factor `ft = |Na| / lerp(s, 1, |Na|)` with adjusted exponent `p = ft·s` and normalization `(p+2)/8·(N·H)^p`; CLEAN's variance measure is `σ² = (1−|Na|)/|Na|`. Both express: **shortened average normal ⇒ raise roughness / lower exponent**. Without mips, compute σ² analytically: the truncated/faded micro-octaves and sub-row wave components have known slope variance (Σ ½(Aᵢkᵢ)² per dropped component; same quantity Cox–Munk measures) — fold that into the specular exponent per row. This is the mobile-feasible LEAN-style AA: variance from math, not textures. https://blog.selfshadow.com/2011/07/22/specular-showdown/ ; https://developer.download.nvidia.com/gameworks/events/GDC2016/akaplanyan_specular_aa.pdf
- **Flow-aligned anisotropic micro-ripples**: stretch the micro-noise domain along the wave-propagation direction (compress across it) so detail reads as wind-combed; with the Cox–Munk σa/σc split (Q1) this doubles as the anisotropic roughness input. (Domain anisotropy per IQ noise article above; slope-statistics justification per Ocean Optics Web Book.)

## Q4. Color depth from ONE identity color

- **Physics to imitate**: water absorbs red first (few meters), then orange/yellow; blue-green survives — deep water is not "darker base color," it is base color **pushed toward the blue-green side and darkened nonlinearly** (Beer–Lambert per channel, which the app already runs in AGSL). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_of_water , https://manoa.hawaii.edu/exploringourfluidearth/physical/ocean-depths/light-ocean
- **Principled OKLab derivation of a deep + subsurface pair from an arbitrary identity hue**:
  - Deep variant: L × 0.55–0.75, **C × 1.1–1.25** (chroma boost when darkening keeps the color alive instead of muddy — design-system practice and pixel-art shading practice agree), hue rotated **≤ 5–10° toward the water side** (toward blue-green for warm hues; near-zero rotation for already-cool hues). OKLCH mechanics: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/color_value/oklch , https://oklch.net/
  - Subsurface variant: L × 1.1–1.2, C × 0.8–0.9, hue rotated a few degrees toward green — "sunlit thin water." (This mirrors Sea of Thieves' deep/subsurface pair, but derived instead of hand-picked; SoT PDF in Q2.)
  - Art practice cross-check (why chroma up + tiny hue shift when darkening reads "expensive"): hue-shifting guides — darker + slightly more saturated + hue nudged cool "works for just about any color"; value-only shading reads washed out. https://www.blue-canary.net/miniature-painting/painting-tips-and-guides/hue-shifting/ , https://kuroonehalf.com/2022/08/07/hue-shifting-tutorial/
  - Tastefulness bound: keep ΔH under ~10° and blend in OKLab (the app already mixes in OKLab); beyond that the note's identity color visibly drifts — violating the identity red line.
- **Keeping saturated identity colors inside gamut when lightening/whitening**: naive per-channel clamp distorts hue; Björn Ottosson's OKLab gamut clipping projects toward a point on the L axis with an adaptive strategy (recommended "adaptive L₀, α=0.05") — the right post-mix safeguard before quantization. https://bottosson.github.io/posts/gamutclipping/
- **Scattering-based gradient vs naive darkening**: SoT blends deep↔subsurface by view angle + sun + crest mask (Q2), i.e. the two-color axis is *lighting-driven*, not just depth-driven; naive depth-only darkening is what makes cheap water look like a gradient fill. (SoT PDF.)
- **How stylized games tint water while keeping art direction unified** (survey):
  - *Wind Waker*: flat base + voronoi-net **bright thin lines** ("the mesh of a fishing net, not the holes"), two scrolling grids at different scale/rotation to kill tiling; 3 colors total. https://danielilett.com/2020-04-05-tut5-3-urp-stylised-water/ , https://godotshaders.com/shader/wind-waker-water-no-textures-needed/
  - *Animal Crossing: New Horizons*: 3 colors lerped with smoothsteps; shore waves as shaped fractional lines animated by sine; foam isolated with smoothstep masks. https://feargrieve.wordpress.com/portfolio/beach-waves-shader/
  - *Zelda BotW/TotK*: SSR-style reflections plus strong surface contrast; still a **small palette with lighting-driven variation**. https://gamingbolt.com/the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-graphics-analysis-how-big-of-a-leap-is-it
  - *2D indie practice* (Cyanilux 2D water: pixelated highlights, distortion, simplified shoreline): https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/2d-water-shader-breakdown/ ; general stylized-water reference: https://ameye.dev/notes/stylized-water-shader/
  - Common denominator: **2–4 derived swatches of one family + one white accent**, with all variation carried by *where* each swatch appears (lighting/geometry masks) — exactly compatible with the app's identity-color constraint.

## Q5. Motion beauty at calm-pond scale

- **Spectrum shape**: capillary↔gravity transition at λ ≈ 1.7 cm; capillary group velocity > phase velocity, gravity-wave group velocity = ½ phase velocity — so ripple packets *outrun* their crests at small scale and *lag* them at larger scale; reproducing both regimes' group/phase ratio in the packet system is a subtle realism cue. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave
- **Fetch-limited (pond) spectra**: JONSWAP sharpens Pierson–Moskowitz with fetch dependence; short fetch ⇒ **higher peak frequency, narrower spectrum, sharper peak** — a pond should not contain low-frequency swell energy; its energy should cluster near a single peak with modest spread. https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Oceanography/Introduction_to_Physical_Oceanography_(Stewart)/16:_Ocean_Waves/16.4:_Ocean-Wave_Spectra , NOAA parametric fetch-limited model: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62217/noaa_62217_DS1.pdf
- **Phase decorrelation**: Tessendorf-style ocean synthesis assigns independent random phases per frequency and lets dispersion (ω=√(gk)) decorrelate them — visible repetition/looping is the CG tell. Refs: SoT cites [Tessendorf 2001] (PDF in Q2); implementation reference: https://github.com/iamyoukou/fftWater . The app's 9 sinusoids + packets already avoid common-period looping if frequencies are non-rational multiples; worth auditing.
- **1/f (pink) amplitude breathing**: pink-noise-modulated micro-motion is *perceived as more natural* than white or no jitter (eye-movement synthesis study; pink noise selected as most natural): https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~sjoerg/docs/Duchowski15_PinkNoise.pdf ; 1/f arises generically from slow random amplitude modulation of an oscillator — i.e., summing 3–4 octave-spaced slow LFOs with random phases onto global amplitude/gust cadence produces it cheaply: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-34816-2
- **Perception research (what the eye actually checks)**:
  - Kawabe et al., PNAS: dynamic image deformation alone produces liquid perception, and the deformation "does not have to precisely simulate refraction" — magnitude and spatial scale of deformation are the operative cues. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1500913112
  - Kawabe, Frontiers in Psychology: a deforming region **lacking a discrete contour** biases perception toward liquid — contour deformation >0.54° visual angle, or contour blur σ ≥ 0.18°, flips percepts toward liquid. Directly supports the app's edge-feathering, and gives a *quantitative* target for how much silhouette softness matters. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01014/full
  - Linear motion coverage as a determinant of transparent-liquid perception (flow smoothness matters): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6305949/
- **Wind-gust spatial texture**: cat's paws are the physical gust texture (capillary patches that darken by tilting sky reflection away) — the app has them; the upgrade is coupling (Q1) and pink-noise scheduling (above). https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2017/11/cats-paws-on-water/

## Q6. Edge/boundary beauty

- **Far edge / horizon of a small water body**:
  - Perspective + Fresnel make the far band the brightest, most sky-like strip (Q1 sources: pscolour.eu, freshscientific.org) — a *lightening* merge into the background, never a dark cut, matches both physics and the app's "far darkness must be faint" red line.
  - Kawabe's contour result (Q5) justifies **feathered/blurred far silhouettes** as literally increasing perceived liquidity — worth treating feather amount as a tunable "liquidness" parameter rather than a fixed constant.
  - Stylized practice: Sable builds depth reading in flat-shaded scenes from **layers + fog + lines** rather than value ramps: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/for-i-sable-i-developing-an-evocative-art-style-comes-first ; GDC talk: https://gdcvault.com/play/1027721/The-Art-of-Sable-Imperfection
- **Near edge (meniscus / contact)**:
  - Physics: the meniscus at a wetting boundary appears **mostly dark with occasional bright lines** depending on angle (refraction at the curved contact surface): https://aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aic.15341 (wetting-edge optics) — i.e., the tasteful treatment is a ~1px darker line of the *identity* color hugging the near rim with an intermittent brighter filament above it, not a grey shadow (grey contact shadows already rejected).
  - Game practice: water-edge darkening at contact + "wet line" on the shore: https://mtnphil.wordpress.com/2012/09/23/water-shader-part-3-deferred-rendering/ ; shoreline construction breakdown: https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/shoreline-shader-breakdown/
- **Rim light**: a faint bright rim on the far edge only when the implied sun is low (ties into towards-sun SSS mask from Q2) reads as backlighting without introducing a new color — SoT's "large low sun" area specular is the big-water version (SoT PDF, Q2).

## Q7. Tone / finish — cheap per-pixel filmic touches

- **Hue-preserving highlight compression (best fit for identity color)** — Khronos **PBR Neutral** tone mapper (2024, built for e-commerce color fidelity — the same "brand color must survive" problem): linear up to a threshold, then highlight compression `pₙ = 1 − (1−Ks)²/(p + 1 − 2Ks)` with `Ks = 0.8 − F₉₀`, then a **controlled desaturation toward white** with gain `g = 1/(Kd(p−pₙ)+1)`, `Kd = 0.15`; guaranteed **zero hue shift** (all changes in the plane spanned by the color and the white axis); ~13 lines of GLSL, trivially portable to AGSL. Spec: https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md ; interactive comparison: https://modelviewer.dev/examples/tone-mapping ; SIGGRAPH talk: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3641233.3664313
- Why not plain Reinhard: luminance-only mapping leaves saturated colors saturated when they should path-to-white; per-channel mapping shifts hue — the classic dilemma summarized at https://bruop.github.io/tonemapping/ . The GT/Uchimura curve (linear mid-section, adjustable toe/shoulder) is the other practical family if a contrast-shaping S-curve is ever wanted: https://cdn2.gran-turismo.com/data/www/pdi_publications/PracticalHDRandWCGinGTS_20181222.pdf
- **Bloom alternatives without a blur pass**: analytic halos — draw each glint's glow as a mathematical falloff (inverse-square-with-offset or Gaussian bell) around its tracked position, composited *before* tone mapping so the tone map rolls the peak — the "pre-widened soft highlight" idea, physically the same trick as halo shaders: https://stemkoski.blogspot.com/2013/07/shaders-in-threejs-glow-and-halo.html ; commercial glow tools use exactly diffuse inverse-square falloff: https://www.maxon.net/en/product-detail/red-giant/universe/glow . The app's AGSL soft bell-profile bands are already this pattern — extending it to glint halos is incremental.
- **If a true blur is ever justified**: Dual-Kawase is the mobile-optimal blur (down/upsample chain; fastest on Mali in ARM's SIGGRAPH 2015 measurements): https://blog.frost.kiwi/dual-kawase/ , https://community.arm.com/cfs-file/__key/communityserver-blogs-components-weblogfiles/00-00-00-20-66/siggraph2015_2D00_mmg_2D00_marius_2D00_slides.pdf ; on Android 12+, `RenderEffect.createBlurEffect` runs the blur in the HW render pipeline (offscreen render + blur + composite; re-runs only on invalidation): https://medium.com/androiddevelopers/blurring-the-lines-4fd33821b83c , chaining/downscale tricks: https://medium.com/androiddevelopers/rendernode-for-bigger-better-blurs-ced9f108c7e2 — but for ~a dozen glints, analytic halos are cheaper and blur-free (aligns with 宁少勿烂).
- **Grain/vignette**: the app already ships triangular-noise dither (the correct anti-banding grain; craft reference: https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/the-art-of-dithering-and-retro-shading-web/ ). Additional film grain on a *calm dialog widget* fights the "calm companion" charter (grain = temporal noise everywhere); a vignette is redundant at 280×420dp where the dialog chrome already frames the water. Verdict: skip both; spend the budget on the tone curve.

## Q8. HDR / wide gamut on Android for this dialog

- **HDR headroom**: Android 15+ exposes `window.colorMode = COLOR_MODE_HDR` + `desiredHdrHeadroom` (recommended ~1.5–2× for mostly-SDR mixed scenes, 5–8× for full HDR); explicitly warned: do **not** brighten SDR content, and HDR next to SDR UI makes the surrounding UI *perceptually dimmer* (simultaneous contrast) — a recording dialog floating over a notes UI is exactly the risky mixed case. https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/09/hdr-and-user-interfaces.html , background: https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/08/what-is-hdr.html
- The UI-toolkit path (`extendedRangeBrightness`) still composites primarily in 8-bit space, tailored per display — workable but delicate; mixed SDR/HDR composition has documented dimming/power interactions at the compositor level: https://source.android.com/docs/core/display/mixed-sdr-hdr
- **Battery**: peak-brightness HDR regions raise panel power (displays are 30–60% of device power; HDR peaks of 600–1500 nits draw significantly more): https://us.ktcplay.com/blogs/technology-hub/hdr-monitor-power-consumption (plus AOSP notes above). For a long-running *recording* dialog, sustained extra nits are a real cost.
- **Wide color gamut (Display P3)** is the practical win instead: API 26+ `COLOR_MODE_WIDE_COLOR_GAMUT` (manifest `colorMode="wideColorGamut"` or `setColorMode`); Canvas/View rendering is color-managed, and saturated identity colors genuinely render more saturated on P3-capable OLEDs. https://developer.android.com/training/wide-color-gamut , color-management background (Nick Butcher): https://medium.com/google-design/android-color-management-what-developers-and-designers-need-to-know-4fdd8054557e . AGSL integrates with the pipeline's automatic color-space conversion (colors are converted into the destination's working space): https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/graphics/agsl
- **Verdict**: P3 window color mode = low-risk, cheap, visible on saturated identity hues (screenshots stay color-managed since it's still SDR). HDR headroom for specular pop = technically possible on Android 15+, but conflicts with the calm charter (glints would out-bright the host app's white UI), risks screenshot/mixed-composition oddities and battery — file as "experiment later, off by default."

## Q9. Art-direction canon — named tricks that transfer to a heightfield ribbon

- **Wind Waker**: identity = flat base + **thin bright wandering lines** (voronoi-net edges, two grids at different scale/rotation/scroll to avoid tiling). Transfers as: sparse bright filaments of lightened identity color riding the surface. https://danielilett.com/2020-04-05-tut5-3-urp-stylised-water/ , https://godotshaders.com/shader/wind-waker-water-no-textures-needed/
- **teamLab "Black Waves"**: 3D particle-simulated water rendered as **lines along particle trajectories** on the wave surface — explicitly modeled on premodern Japanese painting where water is "a series of lines" that "give the impression of life." This is the modern digital execution of the Ma Yuan《水图》idea the charter cites — line-work *as* the water's life, not a texture. https://www.teamlab.art/w/blackwaves/ , spatial philosophy: https://www.teamlab.art/e/ultraspace/
- **Okami**: sumi-e look built from **ink-density variation — more ink on silhouettes than interiors** — and stroke-thickness variation; i.e., contour-weighted darkening of the *same* pigment, never a second hue.
